# FraudLens Research Plan

## 1. Research Objective

To study and evaluate existing technologies and approaches used in financial fraud detection and investigation, identify their functional and technical limitations, and assess the applicability of selected technologies for developing an enhanced financial fraud investigation system.

## 2. Research Questions

### RQ1
What technologies and approaches are currently used for financial fraud detection and investigation?

### RQ2
What capabilities do existing approaches provide for transaction analysis, anomaly detection, entity relationship analysis, evidence handling, investigation support, and reporting?

### RQ3
What functional and technical limitations or gaps exist in existing financial fraud detection and investigation approaches?

### RQ4
Which technologies and approaches can potentially address the identified gaps in financial fraud investigation?

### RQ5
How applicable are the selected technologies and approaches for developing an enhanced financial fraud investigation system?

## 3. Research Areas

### 3.1 Financial Fraud Detection
Study approaches used to identify fraudulent or suspicious financial activity.

Key areas:
- Rule-based fraud detection
- Statistical methods
- Machine learning
- Anomaly detection
- Behavioral analysis

### 3.2 Financial Transaction Analysis
Study how financial transactions are analyzed during fraud detection and investigation.

Key areas:
- Transaction pattern analysis
- Transaction history
- Temporal analysis
- Transaction velocity
- Suspicious transaction identification

### 3.3 Entity and Relationship Analysis
Study how relationships between entities are identified and analyzed.

Key areas:
- Person-account relationships
- Company-account relationships
- Entity resolution
- Transaction networks
- Graph-based analysis
- Fund-flow analysis

### 3.4 Document and Evidence Analysis
Study technologies used to process investigation documents and financial evidence.

Key areas:
- OCR
- Document classification
- Information extraction
- Evidence retrieval
- Document metadata
- Evidence management

### 3.5 AI-Assisted Investigation
Study how AI can support investigators in analyzing information and retrieving evidence.

Key areas:
- Large Language Models
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
- Evidence-grounded question answering
- AI-assisted investigation
- Agent-based systems

### 3.6 Investigation Support and Reporting
Study technologies that support investigators throughout the investigation workflow.

Key areas:
- Case management
- Timeline analysis
- Investigation findings
- Investigation workflows
- Report generation
- Visualization

## 4. Research Sources

### 4.1 Academic Research Sources

The literature survey will primarily use established academic databases and scholarly search platforms.

Primary sources:
- IEEE Xplore
- ACM Digital Library
- ScienceDirect
- SpringerLink
- Google Scholar

These sources will be used to identify research papers related to financial fraud detection, transaction analysis, anomaly detection, graph-based analysis, document intelligence, RAG, LLMs, and AI-assisted investigation.

### 4.2 Official and Industry Sources

Official documentation and industry sources will be used to study existing financial fraud investigation technologies and software systems.

Sources may include:
- Official product documentation
- Official company websites
- Regulatory and government publications
- Banking and financial institution reports
- Standards and technical documentation

### 4.3 Supporting Sources

Additional credible sources may be used when required to understand specific technologies or concepts.

Examples:
- Technical documentation
- Standards organizations
- Government reports
- Institutional publications

### 4.4 Source Selection Principle

Priority will be given to:
1. Peer-reviewed academic research
2. Official regulatory/government sources
3. Official technology or product documentation
4. Reputable institutional and industry sources

Sources will be recorded with sufficient bibliographic information and evidence location to support later comparison and gap analysis.

## 5. Paper and Existing-System Selection Criteria

### 5.1 Inclusion Criteria for Research Papers

A research paper may be included if it:

- Addresses financial fraud detection, financial fraud investigation, or a closely related financial crime problem.
- Studies transaction analysis, anomaly detection, behavioral analysis, or suspicious activity detection.
- Studies entity relationships, graph-based analysis, transaction networks, or fund-flow analysis.
- Addresses document processing, information extraction, evidence analysis, or related investigation tasks.
- Studies AI, machine learning, deep learning, LLMs, RAG, or agent-based approaches relevant to financial investigation.
- Provides sufficient information about its methodology, data, experiments, or findings.
- Is published through a credible academic or scholarly source.
- Contributes information relevant to one or more FraudLens research questions.

### 5.2 Exclusion Criteria for Research Papers

A paper may be excluded if it:

- Has no meaningful connection to financial fraud or financial investigation.
- Focuses on an unrelated fraud domain without applicability to financial investigation.
- Does not provide sufficient methodological or research information.
- Is a duplicate of another selected paper.
- Is an unreliable or unverifiable source.
- Provides information that is not relevant to the research questions.

### 5.3 Selection Criteria for Existing Systems

An existing system or technology may be studied if it:

- Provides financial fraud detection or investigation capabilities.
- Provides transaction monitoring or analysis.
- Supports anomaly or suspicious activity detection.
- Provides entity, network, or relationship analysis.
- Supports document or evidence analysis.
- Provides investigation workflow or case-management capabilities.
- Provides AI/ML-based assistance relevant to financial investigation.
- Has sufficient publicly available information for evaluation.

### 5.4 Source Quality Principle

The credibility and relevance of each source will be considered before including it in the research.

Academic claims will preferably be supported by peer-reviewed research, while capabilities of existing software systems will preferably be supported by official documentation or other authoritative sources.

### 5.5 Relevance to FraudLens

Each selected paper or system should contribute to at least one of the following:

- Understanding an existing approach
- Identifying a capability
- Identifying a limitation
- Understanding a technology
- Supporting a research gap
- Evaluating technology applicability

## 6. Research Extraction Method

Research findings will be recorded using structured extraction tables maintained separately from this research plan.

### 6.1 Literature Extraction

A structured Excel table will be used to record information from selected research papers, including bibliographic details, research problem, objectives, methodology, technologies, datasets, results, limitations, FraudLens relevance, research questions addressed, evidence location, and source links.

### 6.2 Existing-System Extraction

A separate structured table will be used to record information about existing financial fraud detection and investigation systems, including their purpose, capabilities, technologies, investigation features, limitations, and relevance to FraudLens.

### 6.3 Evidence Recording

Important findings will be recorded with their source and evidence location, such as page number, section, table, figure, or official documentation reference, wherever applicable.
